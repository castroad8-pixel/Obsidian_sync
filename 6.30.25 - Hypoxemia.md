Oxygen goals
	No clear consensus
	Best data for those "at risk for hypercapnia"
	Special populations for specific patient populations: 
		Pregnancy: SpO2 > 94-95% (to assist fetal oxygenation)
		Severe sepsis/septic shock: SpO2 94% (hypoperfusion and increased metabolic demand)
		Acute coronary syndrome: SpO2 > 94% (risk of vasoconstriction/worsening ischemia)
		Sickle cell + acute chest syndrome: SpO2 > 92% (or <3% below individual's baseline)
		- Fair to give supplemental oxygen to focal areas of hypoxemia 


"At risk for hypercapnia"
	Obesity hypoventilation syndrome, COPD, Neuromuscular weakness

Excessive oxygen administration in COPD (i.e. drivers of oxygen-induced hypercapnia)
	Reversal of regional hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction, increasing dead space
	Reduced respiratory drive
	Haldane effect (displacement of CO2 from red cells exposed to higher arterial oxygen)

Home oxygen therapy
	For OSA: can prolong respiratory pauses, worsen hypercapnia, doesn't improve AHI/sleep architecture
	Ambulatory hypoxemia: per ATS guidelines, improvement in exercise capacity but **not** in quality of life, effects on dyspnea during exercise testing inconsistent

learning paitents: acute resting hypoxemia: goal < 88, < 92% - 
chronic resting hypoxemia - mortality benefit (for > 88%)

Hyperoxia - harmful effects
